Why this matters for fall color +

Repeated cool nights are part of the seasonal transition that accompanies fall color progression. Elevation strongly influences how quickly these colder conditions arrive across Western North Carolina.

Cool-night and frost signals describe color-development weather. Hard freezes, heavy rain and strong wind are tracked separately as leaf-drop weather because they can eventually increase damage and leaf loss.

How this estimate is calculated +

The source analysis regressed 17 peak-color seasons against September daily mean temperature for 2008–2025, excluding hurricane-disrupted 2024. Its equation is October day = 1.9015 × September mean °F − 106.42, with R² = 0.62 and p < .001.

A planning signal, not a predictive confidence interval. The relationship is sensitive to the unusually warm 2018 and 2019 seasons and does not quantify an elevation adjustment outside the calibrated band.

Rainfall and drought were not used to move the date because the source analysis found no significant timing relationship. They still matter for leaf loss, color quality and the day-to-day viewing experience.
